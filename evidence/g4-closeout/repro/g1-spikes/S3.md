## Practical consequences for the FR-306 envelope

| Fact | unbound | bound |
|---|---|---|
| `transactionHash()` | "unavailable: Transaction hash is available for proven, signed and bound transactions only." | "25bc5fe19e33957d5947a1066fffbe46ab2952e252675a5bf833a26cafd3a392" |
| `fees(initialParameters)` in SPECKs | 834100000000001 | 837350000000001 |

**The unbound form has no canonical transaction hash** — `transactionHash()` is defined only for proven,
signed AND bound transactions. So an unbound offer cannot be named by a chain identifier, which is exactly
why FR-306 content-addresses the envelope by **SHA-256 of the raw `Transaction.serialize()` bytes**. That
is not a stylistic choice: for the form D-306 selects it is the only stable name available. It is also the
right one, since `finalizeRecipe` merges the offer into a LARGER transaction whose hash the maker cannot
know in advance.

**The offer's own `fees()` figure is not the settlement fee.** It is read here only to show the maker
attaches no DUST of its own; the fee that is actually paid belongs to the merged transaction the taker
submits, and spike S6 (Plan 02) measures that.
