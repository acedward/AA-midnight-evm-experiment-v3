### In words

- **A same-colour pool write kills a live offer.** Observed: the offer was refused with `239` (239 — NOT DECODED at these pins). The mechanism is that the maker's call pins the pooled coin it spends — the coin's Merkle index enters the call's transcript — so a deposit that merges the pool changes exactly what the transcript asserted.
- **Age alone does not.** 1/1 untouched offer(s) settled at ages 1800 s. So the offer's lifetime is bounded by ACTIVITY on its colours and by its intent TTL, not by elapsed time as such.
- **Only ONE timing arm is possible per Manager, and that is itself a finding (F-310).** A timing arm must SETTLE to answer its question, and a settlement adds the custody cell that exhausts the publishability budget — after which no further offer can be built at all. So the longest wait is run, because it subsumes the shorter ones. **Gate run 1 separately measured T60 = ACCEPTED, 3/3**, before F-310 was understood; that reading stands and is cited rather than re-run.
- **The TTL ceiling is the ledger's, not this project's:** `global_ttl` = 3600 s, and midnight-js hardcodes `ttlOneHour()` for every intent it builds (`midnight-js-contracts/dist/index.mjs:990`). An offer therefore cannot be published with a longer life, whatever the envelope says.

## Making expiry observable — a technique Plan 03 needs

Because every intent gets a hardcoded one-hour TTL, observing node-side expiry would cost an
hour of waiting per observation. The SHORT-TTL arm rewrites the intent's `ttl` while the
transaction is still UNPROVEN and then proves it, which brings the expiry negative inside a
few minutes. Doing it before proving is not incidental: finding F-306 established that
rewriting a PROVEN transaction's intents invalidates its zswap proofs (`Custom error: 235`,
12/12, including on transactions that would have been accepted untouched).
